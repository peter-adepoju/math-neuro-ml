Mathematical Sciences for Brain Data

A seven-week online bridge course in the mathematics behind neural data analysis and machine learning: linear algebra, calculus, probability and differential equations, learned in Python on real recordings.

Live sessions on Fridays and Saturdays, 5 February to 20 March 2027.

Browse the lessons Course repository

Seven weeks, two sessions a week

Each session teaches one or two lessons. Every lesson has a teaching notebook, an exercise notebook and study notes, and ends with a mini-lab on real data.

Who the course is for

Undergraduate and early postgraduate learners in neuroscience, psychology, biomedical science, data science, engineering, computer science, mathematics and related fields, anywhere in the world. The course is run by ACNEI and taught online.

You should be comfortable with arithmetic, elementary algebra, graphs and scientific notation, and write Python at an intermediate level. You do not need any previous course in linear algebra, calculus, probability or differential equations.

By the end you will be able to

  • describe stimuli, neural responses and datasets as vectors and matrices, with explicit shapes and units
  • fit and analyse linear models with least squares, eigenvalues, covariance and PCA
  • use derivatives, gradients and integrals to study sensitivity, accumulation and learning
  • describe variability and uncertainty with probability, Bayes' rule and the bootstrap
  • simulate and interpret differential-equation models of membranes, spiking neurons and populations
