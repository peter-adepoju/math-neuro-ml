You can read every lesson without an account. This page applies only if you create one.

## What is stored

When you create an account, the site stores:

- **Your name and email address.** You give these when you sign up, or Google shares them if you choose "Continue with Google". Nothing else from your Google account is requested.
- **The lessons you mark as complete**, with the date of each.
- **Your answers to the self-check questions:** whether your first and most recent answers were correct, how many attempts you made, and when.
- **Marks entered by your tutors** for exercises and the project, with any comment they add.

Passwords are handled by the sign-in service and are never visible to the course team.

## Who can see it

You can see all of your own records on the **My progress and scores** page. The course instructor and tutors can see every learner's records so that they can follow progress and enter marks. Other learners cannot see your records.

Your records are not sold, shared for advertising, or used for anything other than running this course.

## Where it is kept

Account records are stored with [Supabase](https://supabase.com), a hosted database service. The website itself is hosted by GitHub Pages, and some of its scripts are delivered by the jsDelivr network. Like most web hosts, these services may keep short-lived technical logs, such as IP addresses, to run and protect their systems.

The site uses your browser's local storage to keep you signed in and, if you are not signed in, to remember the lessons you have marked as complete on that device. It does not use advertising or tracking cookies.

Opening a notebook in Google Colab takes you to Google's service, which has its own [privacy policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy).

## Removing your data

To have your account and all its records deleted, contact the course creator, Peter Adepoju, through the [course repository](https://github.com/peter-adepoju/math-neuro-ml). Deleting an account removes your profile, progress, self-check answers and marks.
